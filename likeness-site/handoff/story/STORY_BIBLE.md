# LIKENESS : THE GAME — Story Bible v001 · "A Night for Four"

Status: **draft for Brandon's sign-off.** Story canon belongs to the HUMAN lane. This is
the first full story pass: CLOUD wrote it, and Brandon approves, changes or rejects it.
The screenplay is [`A_NIGHT_FOR_FOUR.fountain`](A_NIGHT_FOR_FOUR.fountain). How it
becomes the game is in [SCREENPLAY_TO_GAME.md](SCREENPLAY_TO_GAME.md).

It is built on what is already canon: the site's north star ("A room for two. A night for
four." / "The room remembers what love edits out."), the identity locks in
`handoff/data/character_registry.csv`, the five inspect props, the six-beat arc on the
site, Room 14's registered story function ("argument loop, identity fracture"), the
`/street` cast, and the Tape 03 doctrine (slow burn, **no combat**, no restart screen,
only blackouts).

---

## 1 · The one line

**Two people who never existed fall in love in a motel room that was built to replace
them.**

**Logline.** At the Dellwood motel, Room 14 keeps whatever you can't let go of. THOREAU
arrives to settle the night his relationship with DRYA ended. He discovers that the man he
has been defending is someone else, that the woman across from him was built to forgive
that man, and that the only way to stay together is never to agree on what happened.

**Genre.** A twisted love story. Chamber horror, psychological, surreal, nightmarish.
Cerebral and character-driven. The fear comes from timing, evidence and intimacy, never
from a fight.

## 2 · The truth under the story (production spoiler layer)

The audience assembles this from evidence. Nobody ever states it outright, and the device
is never explained.

| | Who | What they did |
|---|---|---|
| **The real THOREAU** (*THE OTHER*, canon `CHR_DARK_THOREAU_001`) | A man who recorded everything and edited their tapes so he would come out as the reasonable one | On the night (the apartment, 3:14 AM) DRYA found his edit, **TAPE 03 — FINAL CUT**. She told him "You love the version of me you can rewind" and left. He ran after her with the camera, fell, and woke on the carpet with the left-temple wound (TAPE 03's cold open, seen from the other side). Later he rented Room 14 and told the device the DRYA who would forgive him |
| **The real DRYA** (*the woman at the crosswalk*) | A dancer. The one thing she never let him film was her dancing | She left. Before she left Dellwood, she rented Room 14 and told the device about the man she had met at a Route 9 bus stop *before* the camera: the one who timed her swim with a three-dollar stopwatch |
| **THOREAU** (the player, `CHR_THOREAU_001`) | **Her likeness of him.** The man she wished he had been | He has her memories of the good year, and nothing of the night. He has never been recorded. He has the bruise because that was the last way she saw him |
| **DRYA** (`CHR_DRYA_001`) | **His likeness of her.** Made from everything he wished she had said | The real woman keeps pushing through his edit: she drinks her coffee sweet and winces, she says "No", she remembers things he never told the device. **She is the contradiction source, and she is a person, not a malfunction** |

"A room for two. A night for four." Two originals and two likenesses: the two people in
the room are the only ones who were never there that night.

**Why the mirror lags.** Mirrors show the original. The likeness moves first and the
original's timing arrives late, which is also why THE OTHER's timing is "wrong": he is
real, and real people don't hit their marks. This turns the canon mechanic
(`MECH_MIRROR_LAG_001`) into story evidence.

## 3 · The house rules (the chamber's logic, also usable as design)

Posted in the front office. They are the only rules the story keeps.

1. **Room 14 is a two-person room.** It doesn't care which two.
2. **Mirrors count.** A reflection is an occupant.
3. **Please keep the lamp on.** THE OTHER enters only in the dark. Tungsten is the truth
   you want; the teal seam is the truth you have.
4. **Checkout is when you agree.** Both occupants have to sign the same account of the night.

Two more rules are demonstrated rather than posted:
- **If he sees you, he does another take.** The room rewinds. There's no death and no
  restart screen, only a rewind (Tape 03 doctrine).
- **Nothing is explained; everything is evidenced.** (Tape 03: "Evidence. Not explanation.")

## 4 · Characters

### Inside the chamber (canon IDs)

**THOREAU** · `CHR_THOREAU_001`. Identity lock unchanged: Black man, early 30s,
medium-deep warm brown skin, round gold glasses, mustache and goatee, guarded intelligent
presence. *Story:* measured, dry, early to everything "like he was guarding it." His
interior voice is noir and sparing: metaphor used as armor. He wants to be the reasonable
one in the story, and he is, which turns out to be the horror. **His arc:** from defending
a version of events to choosing a lie out of love, knowing it's a lie.

**DRYA** · `CHR_DRYA_001`. Identity lock unchanged: Black woman, late 20s, warm
medium-brown skin, dancer body language, jewelry and piercing detail, guarded emotional
force. *Story:* precise and direct. She counts ("five, six, seven, eight") and wastes no
steps. She has wry humor and refuses to perform. **Her arc:** from reciting the lines she
was made to say to saying her own. She leads the dance, she makes the final choice, and
she signs the truth. **Dignity lock holds:** she is never decorative and never only a
victim. Her refusals drive the plot.

**THE OTHER** · `CHR_DARK_THOREAU_001`. Same identity, with the wrong timing, posture,
gaze, breath and response delay (canon). *Story:* the real man. Exhausted, practiced,
directorial ("From the top." "Again."). He carries the camcorder with the red light on.
Through the screenplay's eyes he is sympathetic and not forgiven. His control is shown,
never rewarded. **His fate:** locked out of his own edit, finally on time, with nothing
left to record.

## 5 · Themes

- **Editing love.** Each person keeps an edit of the other. The room makes the edits literal.
- **Likeness and consent.** Recording someone isn't the same as seeing them. The thing
  she never let him film is the only thing in town he can't edit.
- **Forgiveness can't be manufactured.** He built someone to forgive him, and the real
  woman inside her refused.
- **The argument as intimacy.** To stay together, the two likenesses must never settle the
  night. Room 14's canon story function, "argument loop," is also the love story.

## 9 · Endings

DRYA always signs **"The night, as it happened."** That is her choice, never the player's.
The player chooses THOREAU's line:

- **STAY** (the screenplay's ending): he signs **"as we remember it."** The lines differ, so
  they haven't agreed, and the room stays occupied. The two likenesses keep arguing,
  tenderly, forever. THE OTHER is locked out of his own edit. The real DRYA crosses
  Route 9 at 3:15, the first time the clock has moved, and leaves Dellwood.
- **OPEN**: he signs **"as it happened."** They agree, and the door opens. THE OTHER walks
  into an empty Room 14: the lamp is on, two cups sit out (one black, one sweet), and side B
  is in the deck. Across Route 9, Otis counts two more passes. It stays ambiguous whether a
  likeness can exist outside the room.

Both endings are ambiguous, as the Tape 03 doctrine requires.

## 10 · Locks for every future pass

- **No combat, no killing, no guards, no bodies.** The Hitman inputs are translated, not imported (see
  `docs/production/INSPIRATION_INTAKE_006.md` §2 and SCREENPLAY_TO_GAME.md §4).
- **No gore.** The head wound is a year-old bruise. Nothing sexual: intimacy means
  proximity, timing and honesty.
- **DRYA dignity lock**: she leads, refuses and chooses.
- **THE OTHER's control is depicted, never rewarded.** The story ends with him unable to say "edit".
- **The device is never explained.** No sci-fi UI, no lore dump; Desmond's hint is as far
  as anyone gets.
- **Story text lives in data or scripts**, never hard-coded in Blueprints or React.
