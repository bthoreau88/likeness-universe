## What the screenplay settles

1. **This film's ending is not ambiguous.** At dawn Drya tells Thoreau her two-year secret in
   the kitchen. They choose, leave the key on the counter, drive away, and sit together on
   their couch at home. **Drya survives the night in LIKENESS.**
2. **Room 14 is described as "bigger than it should be"** (V02, first Room 14 scene), and the
   kitchen doorway later opens onto the hallway, "the one that should not be opening into a
   kitchen." The screenplay itself frames the suite as the room misbehaving.
3. **The hallway is the motel's corridor, not part of Room 14.** It is short with two doors on a
   normal night, multiplies to "four, maybe six" doors during the night, and is back to two at
   dawn.
4. **The third door.** Drya, alone in the multiplied hallway, hears Thoreau's voice behind a
   door, talking to a woman, and the woman laughs with **Drya's own laugh**. She does not knock.
   She does not open it. She walks back to Room 14. At dawn she knocks on the end door (locked)
   and thanks "whoever you were. Whoever I was."
5. **The doubles appear together only once**, in the Room 14 window after the couple leaves, as
   in the bible's Shot 10.

## Open founder question that decides N1

**In SKIN OF ANOTHER, which woman from Room 14 does not survive: DRYA or MIO?**
- *If DRYA:* the woman who returns, or who laughed behind the third door, is the replacement,
  and MIO is the strongest candidate for her. MIO's veiled human and skeletal states rhyme with
  Beni's two states in the SECOND HAND material (regular and skull form, white cloak).
- *If MIO:* the dossier's skeletal collapse on the Room 14 floor is her, and the replacement is
  someone else (possibly the unnamed Unmarked Woman). LIKENESS and SKIN OF ANOTHER then share
  the night without either woman's fate contradicting the other.
